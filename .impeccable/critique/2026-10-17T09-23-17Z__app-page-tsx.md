## Verdict

Signal has a credible morning-briefing point of view: the lead role, ranked queue, restrained palette, and real job-specific actions make it more purposeful than a generic job board. It is presently strongest as a single-user decision tool, but the expanded-role state turns into a small action console and the new Saved/Later/Archive IA is not yet self-explanatory.

## Strengths

- The lead role earns its priority through placement, role scale, fit rationale, and a ready message rather than decorative dashboard widgets.
- The dense list is useful for scanning. Role, company, freshness, tier, and status can be compared quickly.
- The visual system is restrained and coherent across light and dark modes; the orange accent carries action and priority well.

## Priority issues

### P1 — Expanded records ask for too many decisions at once

The visible action set is Copy referral, Find contacts, Apply, Mark applied, Save, Snooze, and Not relevant. Seven simultaneous choices makes a simple role review feel like a CRM workflow and weakens the intended next action.

Suggested fix: retain Copy message, Find people, and Apply as the primary sequence. Move Save, Snooze, and Not a fit behind a compact “Later” / “More” control, and reveal “I applied” after the application action or in the status area.

### P1 — Queue destinations and filters overlap conceptually

Today, Saved, Later, Archive, Outreach, All, Tier A, Tier B, Applied, and Referred are visible navigation concepts. Users have to infer whether Applied is a queue, filter, status, or a destination; Archive conflicts with the current promise that Not relevant simply vanishes.

Suggested fix: make Today / Saved / Later / Outreach the primary destinations. Keep Applied and Referred as filters inside Today, and hide rejected roles by default with an optional recovery surface in Settings or a lightweight “Undo” toast.

### P1 — State is presented as durable without a clear persistence model

The app saves decisions locally, while live jobs arrive from the Sheet. A user can reasonably assume that “Applied” updates their tracker, then later find a mismatch in another browser or device.

Suggested fix: label the state source plainly (“Saved on this device”) until authenticated persistence exists. When account support ships, make the sync state visible and give a retry/error path.

### P2 — The daily briefing is still too tall when a message is long

The lead role requires the role, facts, fit explanation, full message, and a seven-item action row before the ranked queue begins. It is a polished block, but daily users who already know the pattern will want the list sooner.

Suggested fix: keep the lead title and fit visible, but collapse the full message after the first visit or behind “Review referral note”; preserve the prominent Copy action.

### P2 — Product language alternates between human and internal

“Tier A,” “last run,” “ID,” “Archive,” and “Not relevant” read like system labels, while the rest of Signal promises a calm personal assistant.

Suggested fix: explain Tier A once (“Worth a referral attempt”), rename “Find contacts” to “Find a person,” “Mark applied” to “I applied,” and use short, customer-language helper copy where decisions are irreversible.

### P2 — The demo is not discoverable from the normal product entry point

The real `?demo=1` mode is correctly styled as the dashboard, but there is no production entry screen yet that lets a visitor select Google or Guest. The guest notice alone cannot introduce the product to a first-time visitor.

Suggested fix: make the entry screen a real route and use the exact dashboard component beneath it for Guest mode. The only visual difference should be the guest notice and setup invitation.

### P3 — Destructive and reversible actions lack immediate recovery

Snooze, Save, and Not relevant alter the queue immediately, but the toast does not offer Undo. Archive is less discoverable than the action itself.

Suggested fix: show a short toast with Undo for each action; ensure Saved and Later are visible before an item leaves Today.

### P3 — Referral placeholders can produce an awkward first outreach

Messages containing “Hi {Name}” are easy to copy unchanged.

Suggested fix: add a small “Contact name” field or inline reminder before copy, and substitute it in the copied text only after the user enters a name.
